Definitions and notes
  • Economically disadvantaged is the California Department of Education's student group (called “socioeconomically disadvantaged” in the research files and “economically disadvantaged” on the CAASPP reporting site): students eligible for free or reduced-price meals, or whose parents did not complete high school, or who are migrant, homeless, or foster youth. CDE's definition changed in 2022–23, when the number of grade-11 students counted as disadvantaged rose by about a tenth; the two lines are not strictly comparable across that year.
  • LCFF+ high schools are those where more than 75% of enrollment is low-income, English learner, or foster youth (the unduplicated pupil percentage, UPP). Schools are classified once, on their average UPP over 2016–17 to 2019–20, so the two groups of schools do not change from year to year. The comparison uses each school's all-students grade-11 mathematics results. “Students” weights each school by its tested count; “schools” gives every school the same weight. “Same schools every year” keeps only schools with results in all nine years.
  • Within one high school compares the two student groups inside the chosen school. Cells that CDE suppresses (fewer than 11 students) are omitted, so some schools have gaps. Small groups move a lot from year to year.
  • Measures. The mean scale score is CDE's published mean. Distance from standard subtracts the grade-11 mathematics “standard met” cut score, 2628. The two percentile measures place the group's mean score in the statewide distribution of all tested students' scores, using CDE's published percentile tables (the 1st, 10th, 20th, … 90th, and 99th percentiles) with straight-line interpolation between the published points; one version uses the distribution of the same year, the other the 2018–19 distribution throughout. The achievement-level measures are the share of tested students at each of CDE's four levels.
  • Years are spring test administrations, labeled by the calendar year (2019 = school year 2018–19). There was no test in 2020, and 2021 is omitted because testing was optional that year and fewer than half of students took part; the 2021 rows are in the data files.
