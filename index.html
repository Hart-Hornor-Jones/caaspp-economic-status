Definitions

Economically disadvantaged is the California Department of Education's student group (called socioeconomically disadvantaged in the research files): students eligible for free or reduced-price meals, or whose parents did not complete high school, or who are migrant, homeless, or foster youth. The definition changed in 2022–23, when the number of grade-11 students counted rose by about a tenth.

LCFF+ high schools have more than 75% low-income, English-learner, or foster-youth students (the unduplicated pupil percentage), on their 2016–17 to 2019–20 average; the two sets of schools are fixed. Each school enters with its all-students results. “Students” weights schools by students tested; “schools” weights them equally. “Same every year” keeps schools with results in all nine years.

Within one high school compares the two student groups inside the chosen school. Cells CDE suppresses (fewer than 11 students) are left out.

Measures. Distance from standard subtracts the grade-11 mathematics standard-met cut score, 2628. The percentile measures place the group's mean score in the statewide distribution of all tested students, using CDE's published percentile tables (1st, 10th, 20th, … 90th, 99th) with straight-line interpolation between the published points; one uses the same year's distribution, the other the 2019 distribution throughout. Level shares are percentages of tested students.

Years are spring administrations (2019 = school year 2018–19). No test in 2020; 2021 omitted because testing was optional and under half of students took part.

Data: California Department of Education, CAASPP Smarter Balanced results by student group (statewide and school-level research files, 2015–2025), CALPADS unduplicated pupil counts, and CDE scale-score percentile tables.
